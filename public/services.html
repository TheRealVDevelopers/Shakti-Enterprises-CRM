01 / DESIGN & ENGINEER

Begin with the
right questions.

We review the component’s purpose, drawing or reference sample, mating parts and material requirements. Draft, ribs, wall transitions, fastening, appearance and dimensional priorities are discussed before the tool concept is frozen.

The outcome is a practical brief: approved revision, proposed material, critical dimensions, production quantity and an inspection approach that can guide the next stage.

Explore the engineering approach ↗

02 / DEVELOP & REFINE

Build the tool.
Prove the detail.

Tool design, machining, assembly and trials connect the approved part with a working mould. First-article checks identify what needs refinement, whether that is a visible surface, a functional fit or a production issue.

Documented changes and repeat trials help establish the accepted sample and the tool’s production requirements. Repair and maintenance support keep the mould’s identity and history visible.

Explore mould development ↗

03 / PRODUCE & DELIVER

Make consistently.
Deliver with context.

The production plan links the right machine, mould, material and operator. Shift output enters inspection before it becomes accepted stock. Finishing, assembly and packing follow the agreed component requirements.

Delivery quantities, dates and documents are connected to the order. Quality-held pieces remain separate from dispatchable stock so production totals do not become delivery promises before inspection.
