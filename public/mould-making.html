FROM COMPONENT TO CAVITY

A tool built around
the job it must do.

Mould making connects product geometry with a repeatable manufacturing process. At Shakthi Enterprises, tool design, mould development and injection moulding are considered together, so practical production questions enter the conversation early.

01 / ENGINEERED WITH PURPOSE

Function first

We review how the part will fit, move, seal or support its assembly. Critical dimensions and appearance requirements become part of the tool brief.

02 / ENGINEERED WITH PURPOSE

Material aware

Shrinkage, flow, cooling and stiffness differ across materials and grades. The tool is developed around an approved polymer and application requirement.

03 / ENGINEERED WITH PURPOSE

Production ready

Cavity count, expected demand, changeovers, ejection and maintainability influence how the tool works on the production floor.

DESIGN → TOOL → TRIAL

Five stages.
One connected process.

Understand the component

We begin with a drawing, 3D model or reference sample. The intended use, assembly interfaces, appearance, quantity and delivery requirement shape the tooling brief. Open questions are resolved before a design is frozen.

Understand the component

We begin with a drawing, 3D model or reference sample. The intended use, assembly interfaces, appearance, quantity and delivery requirement shape the tooling brief. Open questions are resolved before a design is frozen.

Engineer for manufacture

Draft, wall transitions, ribs, bosses, parting lines and undercuts are reviewed with the proposed material. Gate position, shrinkage and ejection must work together, rather than being treated as separate decisions.

Develop the mould

The approved concept becomes a tool layout with cavity, core, runner, cooling and ejection systems. Machining and finishing focus on the functional surfaces that determine the quality of the part.

Trial, measure and refine

Initial trials establish whether the tool and process can meet the agreed criteria. First-article inspection, assembly checks and visible defects guide documented corrections and repeat trials.

Release and support

Approved samples, tool identity, component revision and process requirements are retained for production. Cleaning, maintenance, shot history and repairs help preserve the tool through its working life.

THE DETAILS INSIDE THE TOOL

Every feature
has a purpose.

01 / ENGINEERED WITH PURPOSE

Core & cavity

Define the internal and external surfaces of the part. Fits, finish and dimensional requirements guide the design and inspection of these working surfaces.

02 / ENGINEERED WITH PURPOSE

Runner & gate

Direct the melt into the cavity. Gate location, filling balance and trimming requirements are reviewed against the component geometry and appearance.

03 / ENGINEERED WITH PURPOSE

Cooling circuits

Manage heat removal through the tool. Cooling balance influences cycle time, shrinkage, distortion and consistency.

04 / ENGINEERED WITH PURPOSE

Ejection system

Releases the cooled part without unacceptable marks or distortion. Pin positions and support are considered alongside draft and part geometry.

05 / ENGINEERED WITH PURPOSE

Guidance & alignment

Guide pillars, bushes and locating features align the tool halves. The assembly must remain stable under the approved production conditions.

06 / ENGINEERED WITH PURPOSE

Serviceable inserts

Where appropriate, inserts make wear areas or design changes easier to maintain. Tool ownership, revision and repair history remain identifiable.
