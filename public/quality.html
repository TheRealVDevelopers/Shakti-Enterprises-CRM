QUALITY STARTS BEFORE PRODUCTION

Agree on what
good looks like.

The drawing, component revision, material specification and agreed sample establish the inspection basis. Critical dimensions, visual criteria and functional checks should be clear before the first production batch.

01 / ENGINEERED WITH PURPOSE

Incoming material

Identify the grade, owner, colour and batch. Received material remains separate from usable stock until the required acceptance checks are complete.

02 / ENGINEERED WITH PURPOSE

First article & process

Review the trial or setup sample against the approved criteria. Changes to tool, revision or process require the appropriate review before release.

03 / ENGINEERED WITH PURPOSE

In-process inspection

Record the production lot and inspection result. Accepted, rejected and held quantities remain distinct so a pending decision cannot be mistaken for released stock.

INSPECT / HOLD / RELEASE

A decision that
follows the part.

Production counts and quality acceptance are different records. Held output needs a documented review or reinspection. Only accepted stock is available for allocation and dispatch in the connected workflow.

Where a customer requires a specific report, gauge, test or traceability format, that requirement belongs in the component brief and inspection plan. Certifications and guaranteed capabilities should only be stated when supporting evidence is available.

BEFORE IT LEAVES

Check the quantity.
Protect the component.

01 / ENGINEERED WITH PURPOSE

Correct identity

Match the component, revision, client order and delivery schedule before the shipment is confirmed.

02 / ENGINEERED WITH PURPOSE

Appropriate packing

Use the agreed piece count, protection and identification. Packing is part of the component’s delivery requirement.

03 / ENGINEERED WITH PURPOSE

Traceable dispatch

Retain shipment quantity, transport reference and acknowledgement so the delivery can be followed through the order.
